# Spawn preferences and observed adaptation

Each fighter receives eleven reproducible, independently seeded preference multipliers from 0.3 to 1.7 for risk, speed, eagerness, defense, items, positioning, jumping, close-range dogfighting, whiff punishment, safe whiff baiting and grappling. Ground preference is the inverse of jumping. These rank legal choices; they never change reaction delay, damage, stamina, movement permissions or combat rules. Older seven-trait saves receive neutral weights for the four new traits.

`observePersonality` reads committed attack starts, completed contactless attempts, visible enemy feints and movement without progress. `observeEvents` reads resolved public contacts, distinguishing hits, blocks and parries. Duplicate events are bounded and ignored. No pending opponent inputs are inspected. No-contact attempts and lack of progress are evidence, not assertions about player intent.

Repeated parries lower that move's score and increase feint preference. Observed enemy feints increase caution about fresh guards. Movement without progress increases route reconsideration. Preferences persist across rounds; round counters and transient observations reset, recent tendencies decay slightly, and move history remains within the match. A new match creates a fresh profile and empty learning history. This is bounded online policy adaptation, not offline training or database ingestion.

Move attempts and outcomes are bounded effective evidence, not lifetime totals. Before any counter would exceed 32, all five counters for that move halve together. This preserves their relative weight and lets recent success or failure keep changing preferences after long matches. Fractional evidence restores exactly; older integer snapshots remain valid. Multi-strike moves can contribute multiple contacts per attempt, so the resulting score is not a hit probability. Round counters remain capped diagnostics. Neither the number of interactions nor a complete touch-of-death string has a separate learned reward: move preferences currently use damaging hits, parries and whiffs only. Zero-damage contacts, including post-KO juggling, still prevent false whiffs but never earn positive hit evidence or reduce parry caution.

The saved state has at most 24 move summaries and 64 recent event IDs. `validation.ts` strictly validates nested fields, finite ranges and bounds, then deep copies the snapshot. `personality.test.ts` covers independent profiles, parry and success learning, whiffs, feints, movement evidence, lifecycle, duplicate feeds, long-match reversal of outcomes, actual post-KO contact attribution and exact continued learning after restore.
